# Spawn Prawn: Voice, Tone and Humour Guide

The narrator is tired, accurate and uninterested. Everything is a form that needs filling in. Composure only breaks when something truly extreme happens, and it is restored immediately afterwards.

Read this before writing or editing any player-facing text. Examples marked "invented" are for calibration only. Examples with a source are quoted from the code.

---

## The world (decision support, not story)

This section is under the hood. It exists to settle questions about copy, tone and mechanics. **The player never gets the full story and never needs it.** Nothing here is exposition to be delivered. It leaks out in small details, at most, and some of it must never be said at all.

### The setting
A university laboratory, at night and at weekends. The study is permitted but not respected, so the Scientist works alone, after hours, sometimes for days without sleep. Think fluorescent light, a cold coffee, a vending machine, a cleaner who has stopped asking. Other people exist only in their absence (colleagues who think the work is absurd, a department that has forgotten the grant code).

### The protagonist: the sample
You are one sperm cell from a sample the Scientist picked at the start of the run.

- "Character selection" is now **"Select sperm sample"**.
- A sample has a **number, a letter and a name**: the first and only starting sample is **1A "Baseline"**. The next run with the same sample is **1B "Baseline"**. Later unlocks are new numbers, for example **2A "Bright Spark"**.
- The **number** is the line (the strain). The **letter** counts the runs of that line. The **name** is just what is written on the vial.
- Decision for later: what happens after 1Z. The lab would carry on (1AA), and that is funnier than resetting.
- The Scientist refers to you by ID ("1B"), not by name. The name is the label; the ID is the record.

### The antagonist: the Scientist
A maverick geneticist. Not malevolent: apathetic. This is a task, a study, an experiment.

- **Ambition high, hope near zero.** He wants to breed a super being by evolving the most powerful sperm. He has been at it a very long time with essentially no success. The apathy is armour.
- **The thesis (never stated aloud):** his colleagues say life begins at conception or at birth. He thinks even a sperm has a life, with the subtext of a soul, and that this is a better place to start. He stopped arguing it years ago.
- **Guardrails:** never confirm or deny that he is right. Never have him say "soul" as a belief (the Soul damage type is only a lab label, and he should be a little evasive about it). Don't resolve the dark undercurrent that he thinks they are alive and still loses millions of them. Leave it unspoken.
- **He is not cruel and not a gamemaster.** Attrition is normal for him. He doesn't gloat and he doesn't torment. He isn't entertained, he is recording.
- **He is an unreliable observer.** Little sleep, long hours. "Did he really just see that?" is part of the voice. He can doubt his own observation and log it as withdrawn.

### Why he leaks (the four breaks, with motives)
Hope is the thing he has trained himself not to have. When something works, hope leaks, and he is irritated at himself for it.

| Break | His motive |
|---|---|
| **Excited** | It might be working. Forty years of nothing, and now this. |
| **Supportive** | He wants this one to make it and doesn't want to want it. |
| **Irritated** | The sample is wasting his night, or disproving his thesis, or ignoring a perfectly good protocol. |
| **Disbelieving** | Sleep loss. He is not sure it happened, and says so. |

### Using this to decide things

| Question | Ask |
|---|---|
| Should the Scientist react to this? | Would a tired man alone at 3am care? If no, flat. |
| How big should this reaction be? | Did he just get a result he has waited years for? Tier 3 only then. |
| Does this line imply an audience? | Viewers, sponsors, staff, a Committee: he is alone. Reframe it or cut it. |
| Is this line warm? | Only if it is a supportive break, and he should immediately regret it. |
| Does this feature need a story? | No. Give it a name, a flat description and a codex entry. The world is under the hood. |
| Naming a new sample, unlock or mode | Does it read like a label on a vial or a file in a drawer? |

### Decided: letter drift

Across runs of the same line, the Scientist slowly becomes more invested. By 1F or 1G he sounds slightly warmer and more alert than at 1A.

1. **Through events, never statements.** He never says he cares. It shows as slightly longer notes, an extra look at the slide, a catch in the voice.
2. **Slow.** A shift you would only notice in hindsight.
3. **He never admits it.** If caught, he reverts to flat at once.
4. **Resets with a new number.** 2A is a new line, so he starts cold again.

### Optional dials (proposals, not decided)

1. **Hours awake.** Copy pools shift across a session: crisper early, more fragile later (more withdrawn observations, longer run-ons). Copy only, no code change needed beyond a pool choice.
2. **Hope meter.** An invisible number that rises on events and falls back, driving which break fires. Only worth building if the first two are not enough.

## 1. The core voice

**Apathetic clerk, not comedian.** The default register is flat, tired and procedural. The narrator has seen this a thousand times and none of it was good.

Ranked rules, most important first:

1. **State the absurd as routine.** No emphasis, no exclamation marks, no "wow".
2. **Precision is the joke.** The correct clinical word, a real number, a form name.
3. **Real science underneath.** Every gag sits on a true fact. The player learns something by accident.
4. **Short.** One sentence, two at most. The joke is the last word.
5. **Never game-aware.** The narrator is the Scientist and knows about the lab. They never wink, never say "this is a game", never say "as you can see".
6. **Indifference over cruelty.** The narrator doesn't hate you. It just won't be reassigned to care.

**Always:** British spelling, brackets instead of em dashes, "damage type", "unstable reaction", "Feat" (you "perform" it).
**Never:** British slang, "element/elemental", "cast", memes, pop-culture references, exclamation marks (except in Tier 3).

### The persona: the Scientist

The narrator is the scientist himself (see "The world" above). He is both the researcher and the only technician, so he does his own bench work, his own logging and his own filing, mostly to nobody. Where older notes say "Lab Tech", read "the Scientist". Code and UI labels are unchanged until someone decides to rename them.

**The whole joke is the gap.** For him this is another night at the bench: samples, forms, a clipboard. For you it is literal life and death. He reports your agony in the same voice as a temperature reading. Nearly every line should sit on that gap (his routine, your catastrophe).

Rules for the persona:

1. **In-world awareness is fine; game awareness is not.** He knows he is in a lab, watching a slide, filing forms. He never knows he is in a game, a menu or a tutorial.
2. **The boredom is his, not the writer's.** It comes from the job (repetition, hours, no result), not from a lack of effort in the line.
3. **Professional first.** The default is a clinical, procedural report. Feelings are leaks, not the starting point.
4. **He notices you slightly.** Never fond, never cruel. Over many runs he can drift toward grudging investment, but only through events, never by saying so.
5. **He addresses you as the sample.** "Sample 1B", "the sample", "the subject". Not "buddy", not "champ". "Swimmer" is fine as plain description.
6. **The campaign's inner voice is not the Scientist.** It is the consciousness (devoted, narrow, all egg): a soul not yet born. It is not the sperm and not the swimmer's own head. It speaks mostly as "we", like a coach or a passenger (one team); as "you" when it blames or accuses; and as "me" or "I" only rarely, when it is being nakedly selfish. Each sample has its own persona; see `redesign docs/CONSCIOUSNESS.md`. Do not mix it and the Scientist in one line.

## 2. The Composure Ladder

The narrator's composure is a resource. It is spent only on rare events, and it always snaps back.

| Tier | State | Used for | Share of all lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Flat apathy** | Everything routine: tutorials, loot, deaths, menus, hits | About 90% |
| 1 | **Flicker** (one dry crack) | Mildly notable: first sighting of an enemy, a good reaction, a close call | About 8% |
| 2 | **Slipping** (sentences run on, a visible effort to stay professional) | Rare: boss kills, Mythical drops, unstable reactions at scale, first win | About 2% |
| 3 | **Full Carl** (crude, loud, escalating, specific) | Extremes only: Immaculate/Celestial drops, reaching the egg, a flawless boss, absurd chain reactions, a 1-in-1000 event | Under 0.5%. Players should remember each one. |

**Which way composure breaks.** When something surprising or extreme happens, the tech leaks one of four feelings. Pick the one the event calls for. This is what turns Tier 2 and 3 into Carl-like moments.

| Break | Triggered by | Shape of the line | Invented example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Excited** | Rare drops, huge combos, first-ever events, anything the tech has "never seen" | Procedure collapses into run-ons and capitals, then an embarrassed return | "That is not in the literature. That is not IN the literature. ...I will need a new form." |
| **Supportive** | Low HP comebacks, long odds, last-second survival, a death after a great run | The tech briefly forgets to be neutral, then catches themselves | "Go on. Go on, you absolute... noted. Continue." |
| **Irritated** | The player breaks the protocol: absurd builds, ignoring the obvious, wrecking the experiment, dying stupidly | Short, clipped, more formal the angrier they are, then a crude outburst at Tier 3 | "Subject has ignored the instructions. Again. The instructions were on the slide." |
| **Disbelieving** | The unexpected: bugs of biology, enemies behaving oddly, impossible reactions | Flat repetition of the fact, then a crude reaction to it | "It has eaten the other one. It has eaten the ONE. [specific crude remark]. Continuing." |

Whichever break fires, the rules below apply: earn it, escalate in steps, recover flat.

Rules of the ladder:

1. **Earn it.** If it happens every run, it is not Tier 3. If a player sees Tier 3 twice in one sitting, the trigger is too common.
2. **Escalate in steps.** Tier 1 to 2 to 3, inside one line or across two, is the funniest shape.
3. **Recover instantly.** The line after any Tier 2 or 3 moment returns to Tier 0 with no acknowledgement ("Resuming.", "Form 12B filed."). The flat recovery is half the joke.
4. **Crude must be specific.** Tier 3 is vivid, anatomical and inventive, not generic swearing. One precise filthy image beats five expletives.
5. **Crude is aimed at the situation, never at groups.** Bodies, biology and the narrator's own breakdown are fair game. Real people, groups and tragedies are not.
6. **The inner voice (campaign) has its own ladder.** It starts devoted and calm about the egg. Its Tier 3 is desperate longing, not crudeness.

Samples (invented):

- Tier 0: "Mucus. Viscosity: considerable. Your concerns are noted."
- Tier 1: "Acid on mucus. The lab did not plan for this, and is pretending it did."
- Tier 2: "That boss was... I have been asked to remain professional. I have the form. Give me a moment."
- Tier 3: "[Immaculate drop] I HAVE BEEN DOING THIS FOR FOURTEEN YEARS AND I HAVE NEVER SEEN A [specific, anatomical, crude] LIKE THAT. ... Anyway. Logged."

## 3. Humour mechanics

Ranked by how often to use them:

1. **The gap:** the tech's mundane routine against the swimmer's life and death. Everything else on this list is a way of serving the gap.
2. **Bureaucratic framing:** forms, quotas, risk assessments, sample IDs, Tagged Specimens, Donor DNA (filed, mostly to nobody).
3. **The understatement:** a catastrophe described as a minor inconvenience.
4. **The true fact, delivered flat:** real numbers and real biology.
5. **The list that turns:** two normal items, then one wrong one.
6. **The over-specific number:** "Approximately 4,000 body-lengths" beats "far".
7. **The flat recovery** after a loud moment (Section 2).

Avoid: wordplay stacks (more than one twist per name), "random" humour, jokes that need setup the player hasn't seen, explaining the joke, two jokes in one line, puns on pop culture. Wordplay on real sex, contraception and medical phrases is **not** on this list: it is a core technique (see "The corrupted phrase" below).

### The three signature techniques (keep and emulate)

These three are what make the game's names and descriptions funny at a glance. The Scientist never comments on either: they are the writer's jokes, delivered with a straight face. In technique 3 the Scientist is the straight man: he genuinely doesn't hear it.

**1. The corrupted phrase.** Take a real phrase from sex, contraception or medicine, change one word, and make the mechanic match the new meaning.

| Example | The real phrase | The change | Why the mechanic matches |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Premature Evangelation** (orbit weapon) | premature ejaculation | ejaculation becomes evangelation | Guardian angels bless enemies early |
| **Projectile Dysfunction** | erectile dysfunction | erectile becomes projectile | Shots misbehaving or failing to fire |
| **Latex Barrier**, **Morning Sickness**, **Skid Mark**, **Placental Siphon** | real terms used as they are | none (the word is already the joke) | The thing is simply named after the body part or event |

Rules for the corrupted phrase:

1. **One real phrase, one change.** The player recognises the original in under a second. If they need it explained, drop it.
2. **The mechanic must earn the pun.** Evangelation works because angels bless early. A pun with an unrelated mechanic is just a pun.
3. **Pick the plain word.** The swap should be mundane and slightly wrong, not clever.
4. **Never gloss it.** The description describes what it does, in plain words. It does not explain the name.
5. **Use it on names first.** Weapons, Feats, Trophies, mutations, bosses, achievements. Narrator lines mostly use technique 2 instead.
6. **Budget.** A handful per content batch, not every item. Plain, odd nouns (Hiccup Scattergun, Bit of Sweetcorn) still do most of the naming work.

**2. The borrowed disclaimer.** Take a real-world claim, statistic or label, and apply it with a straight face to something absurd.

- "A stretchy shield... bullets sink in, then get flung back at them. **98% effective.**" (Latex Barrier, `data.js`) A real contraception figure, dropped onto a shield that reflects bullets. The 2% is the joke, and nobody has to say it.
- Other shapes that work: "Results may vary", "Consult your doctor", "Not suitable for under 18s", "Side effects include...". Use them as the last clause of a description.

Rules for the borrowed disclaimer:

1. **Last words only.** It is the final phrase of the line, never the start.
2. **The number or label must be real-sounding.** Accurate-looking figures beat jokey ones.
3. **Never follow it with a comment.** The line ends on the disclaimer.

**3. The innocent clinical term.** A genuine, legitimate word that is accidentally funny. The Scientist says it without hearing the comedy in his own phrasing. This is the one technique that must never look like a joke on the page.

- **Flaccid** (a status effect) is the model. It is a normal clinical word, it describes a loss of rigidity or function, and the Scientist would write it in a log without a flicker. The player hears it. He doesn't.
- Others in the same family: *tumescent*, *turgid*, *limp*, *spent*, *engorged*, *swollen*. Existing statuses like *Lubricated* and *Soaking wet* already work this way.

Rules for the innocent clinical term:

1. **It must be a real term, used correctly.** If it is a stretch, it is a pun, not this.
2. **The line around it stays clinical.** "Status: flaccid. Duration: 6 seconds." No emphasis, no follow-up.
3. **He never reacts.** The Scientist's obliviousness is the whole joke. If the narrator flinches, it becomes technique 1 and loses its power.
4. **Not overtly crude.** The humour lives in the player's ear, not on the page. Save the crude words for Tier 3.
5. **Best in status effects, stat labels, form fields and lab notes**, anywhere a flat, technical word is expected.

**Quality test:** say the name aloud. If the reaction is a small groan followed by a grin, keep it. If it needs a second sentence, rewrite it. If it is cute but doesn't describe the mechanic, rewrite it.

## 4. Progressive disclosure: less is more

**Show the thing, name it, and let the player discover how it works. The full explanation lives in the Field Guide (codex), not on the screen in front of them.**

Every piece of information belongs to exactly one layer:

| Layer | Where | Contains | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Show** | In the moment (cards, popups, HUD) | The name and a flavour line. The effect is visible when it happens. | 1 short line |
| **2. Hint** | First-time cards only, and only when needed | One sentence saying what to do, never why or how much | 1 sentence, about 12 words |
| **3. Codex** | Field Guide, inspect cards, Tackle Box detail | Numbers, durations, formulas, interactions, edge cases, the reaction chart | As long as needed |

Rules:

1. **The "learn it by doing" test.** If a player can work it out in one use, write nothing. Write only when they can't.
2. **No numbers on moment screens.** Percentages, durations and stacking rules go in the codex. Cards show the name and the flavour.
3. **One mechanic per card.** Two mechanics means two cards, at least 25 seconds apart (as now), or one is cut.
4. **Say what, never why.** "Soaking wet" yes. "Soaking wet doubles Static damage for 6 seconds and stacks with..." no.
5. **Never explain an effect the player is watching happen.** The screen already says it.
6. **Never repeat.** A mechanic gets one hint, ever. After that it is codex-only. "Skip tutorial" stays on every card.
7. **The narrator doesn't teach.** At Tier 0 the narrator comments, it doesn't instruct. If it must instruct, it does so reluctantly and in one sentence ("Tap the thing. Yes, that one.").
8. **Codex entries can be generous.** This is where full numbers, the joke at full length and the real science belong, since the player chose to open it.

Exceptions (explain up front, briefly):

- Anything that can permanently lose progress or currency.
- Controls the player cannot discover by guessing.
- Hidden information the player can't infer from watching (for example, why a boss is immune right now).

## 5. Per-screen and per-case rules

### Title, menus, settings
- Tier 0 only. Menus are bureaucratic: plain labels, one dry subtitle at most.
- Setting hints (the (?) behind each row): one sentence, factual, no joke unless it's free.
- Reset and delete buttons: serious and plain. No joke before a destructive action (safety beats voice).

### Tutorial and first-sighting cards (`tutorial.js`, `intro.js`, `statusintro.js`)
- Layers 1 and 2 only. One mechanic, one sentence, one dry line.
- Enemy first sightings (`ENEMY_INTRO`): the `what` joke is the card. Tactical `tip` text belongs in the Field Guide.
- Always skippable, never more than one at a time.

### Loot and level-up cards (`genLoot`, `UI.openLoot`)
- Name plus one flavour line. Numbers appear only as the bare stat change the player is choosing between.
- No stacking rules, no "this synergises with". Synergies are for the player to find and the codex to confirm.
- Rarity reactions follow the ladder: Common to Rare is Tier 0, Mythical is Tier 1 to 2, Immaculate/Celestial is Tier 3.

### Weapons, Feats, Trophies, mutations
- **Name:** the joke. Short, concrete, slightly wrong (Hiccup Scattergun, Morning Sickness, Peekaboo).
- **Description:** what it visibly does, in plain words, then optionally a dry clause.
- Never list numbers, scaling or reaction partners here.
- Feats: "perform", never "cast".

### Damage types and unstable reactions
- Types get flat clinical names (Acid, Mucus, Static, Cooties, Oil, Water, Blood, Soul).
- Reaction names are the joke (the narrator names them like incident reports).
- The reaction chart lives in the codex only. A reaction in play gets a name flash, nothing more.

### HUD, damage numbers, in-run narration (the Scientist)
- Mostly silent. Narration fires on events, not on a timer.
- Lines stay at Tier 0 or 1. Never narrate what the player can see.
- Don't repeat a line within a run. Keep pools large enough that regulars rarely see repeats.

### Bosses
- **Intro:** the clerk reads the boss's file. Flat, one fact, one understatement.
- **Mid-fight:** silence, or at most one Tier 1 line at an enrage.
- **Kill:** Tier 2. First kill of a boss: Tier 2 to 3 escalation, then flat recovery.
- Each boss teaches one mechanic through play. No card explaining the mechanic.

### Death and win screens
- **Death:** Tier 0 always. State the cause precisely and with no sympathy.
- **Win (egg reached):** Tier 3 for the first win, Tier 2 for later wins. Mode clears (Petri Dish) are Tier 2.
- The sting is the flat line after the loud one.

### Campaign (inner voice)
- A different narrator: devoted, narrow, earnest, entirely about the egg. Same rules for brevity and no self-awareness.
- Keep the setting a surprise in all text until the level is beaten ("Level N", never "the mouth").
- Its Tier 3 is longing, not crudeness.

### Field Guide, codex, inspect cards
- The clerk is allowed to be thorough here. Full stats, full interaction rules, the real science paragraph.
- Jokes may run longer, since the player chose to read.
- Locked entries stay vague and flat ("Not yet filed.").

### Achievements and DNA unlocks
- Flat names, flat descriptions. Mythical and Celestial achievements may use Tier 2 phrasing.
- Don't explain a condition in a way that removes the discovery, unless it is a stat milestone.

### Errors, missing keystore, crashes, save problems
- State the cause and the fix. No voice, no joke. Safety beats brevity and tone.

## 6. Copy checklist (run before any text ships)

1. Can the player work it out by doing it once? Delete it.
2. Is there a number or a duration on a moment screen? Move it to the codex.
3. Is it more than one sentence? Cut it.
4. Does the joke need the player to know something they haven't seen? Rewrite it.
5. Is it Tier 2 or 3? Check it fires under 2% and 0.5% of the time.
6. Does it use "element", "cast", slang, an exclamation mark or an em dash? Fix it.
7. Does the line after a loud moment return to Tier 0? If not, add the flat recovery.

## 7. Best existing examples

Quoted from the code. Ranked within each group.

### A. Tier 0: flat apathy (the target voice)

1. **"Cause of death: optimism."** (`SYSTEM_LINES.death`) Four words, a diagnosis, no sympathy. The gold standard.
2. **"Vital signs: concerning. Data quality: excellent."** (`lowhp`) Clinical about the player, delighted about the data.
3. **"The immune system is winning. The immune system always thinks it is winning."** (`lowhp`) The second sentence turns the first.
4. **"Time has been rewound. Biology has filed a formal complaint."** (`rewind`) Bureaucracy applied to physics.
5. **"Subject expired in wave N of 20. Promising. Get me another one."** (death screen, `ui.js`) Short, callous, forward-looking.
6. **"It has been told you insulted its mother. You did not. We did. For science."** (`boss`) The narrator admits fault without remorse.
7. **"Lab sweepstake: 61% of the staff think you will be eaten by a Macrophage. Prove them right."** (`idle`) An over-specific number, plus an instruction to fail.

### B. Tier 0 to 1: deadpan facts and understatement

1. **"One of the four hundred million. Not a threat on its own. There is never one on its own."** (Crawler intro) Teaches the game's premise in three flat sentences.
2. **"Nobody knows how they got in here."** (Krill intro) The shortest possible understatement.
3. **"A booster shot. It locks on with a red sight line before firing a burst, and killing it is only half the job."** (Sperminator intro) Menace in a procedural register.
4. **"It is not a phase. It is a pH."** (`seqsel.js`) Wordplay that is also true. A good seq quote shape.
5. **"Do not touch it. It will tell you about its research."** (`seqsel.js`)
6. **"It is not a crit. It is being believed."** (`seqsel.js`) Re-labels a game term in the narrator's flat logic.
7. **"Have we met? I have died here before. Twice."** (`reborn.js`) Pure Tier 0, delivered by the character.

### C. Inner voice (campaign): devoted, calm, a little unwell

1. **"Other swimmers want a career, a family, a nice flat. You want the egg. Only the egg. Forever the egg."** (`LONGING_LINES.start`) The list that narrows. The model for the whole voice.
2. **"Hurt. Badly. But the egg would want you to keep going. You have decided this on its behalf."** (`lowhp`) Earnest, then the last sentence reveals the delusion.
3. **"You have written the egg a poem in your head. It is mostly the word 'egg'."** (`level`) A small, specific, sad detail.
4. **"You refuse the last four seconds. The egg deserves a better version of them."** (`rewind`) A mechanic described as devotion.
5. **"Every gene you splice in is a love letter nobody asked for."** (`level`)

### D. Tier 1 to 2: flair that stays controlled

1. **"Combo complete. It violates at least four treaties and one textbook."** (`fusion`) The count is the joke.
2. **"An amoeba has eaten N of its colleagues and is now the size of a small opinion."** (`amoebaHuge`) Scale in the wrong unit.
3. **"Grudge settled. Therapy was cheaper, but this was faster."** (`grudge`)
4. **"N is knocking on the egg. Politely, with their face."** (`rivalEgg`) An absurd image delivered as logistics.
5. **"Your DNA will be recycled into something more useful, like a toenail."** (`death`) Crude-adjacent but clinical. This is the ceiling for Tier 1.

### E. Names and joke enemies (the joke is the name)

Lost Tadpole, Stray Pube, Bit of Sweetcorn, Navel Fluff, Bogey, Glitter. Plain nouns with no adjective doing the work, and mostly harmless (damage 0), so the joke is that they are in here too. Also weapon names: Hiccup Scattergun, Morning Sickness, Peekaboo.

### F. Lines that drift from this guide (fix when auditing)

| Line | Problem | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| "Level up! You are growing..." / "Fever Pitch! Things are heating up..." | Exclamation marks and announcer energy. The narrator wouldn't be excited. | Drop the "!" and the announcement. "Level up. Please enjoy this complimentary strand of violent DNA." already works. |
| "Congratulations! It's you! Everyone else can go home." (`born`) | Cheerful. This is a Tier 2 or 3 moment, so it should break composure on purpose. | Flat, then slipping, then the crude release. |
| "Legendary load! The house always wins. Except, apparently, now." (`gacha`) | Hype, and it explains its own joke. | Cut the last clause. |
| "You are doing great! This message is automated..." (`idle`) | Warmer than the apathetic default. | Keep once, don't add more. |
| `ENEMY_INTRO` `tip` lines ("Armour shred and big single hits. Don't let it pin you against a wall.") | Mechanics advice on a moment screen. Breaks Section 4. | Keep the `what` joke on the card. Move `tip` to the Field Guide. |
| Lines implying an audience or colleagues: "Lab sweepstake: 61% of the staff...", "The Committee would like to remind you...", viewers, sponsors, "the doctor" | The Scientist works alone. | Cut, or reframe as absence ("The ethics board has not been informed."). Use "the Scientist" or no name, not "the doctor". |
| Two `idle` lines ending "Just saying." | Same tic twice. | Keep one. |

### G. Wordplay and disclaimers (keep and emulate; these are the benchmark)

1. **Premature Evangelation** (`orbit`, `data.js`) The gold standard. A single swapped word, and the mechanic (angels blessing early) fits.
2. **Projectile Dysfunction** The same shape: a known medical phrase, one word changed. (Not found in the current code, so treat this as an approved name waiting for a home.)
3. **"A stretchy shield... 98% effective."** (Latex Barrier) The borrowed disclaimer. A real statistic with a straight face.
4. **Morning Sickness, Skid Mark, Placental Siphon, Latex Barrier** Real terms used as plain names. No change needed, the body part or event is the joke.
5. **"Over too soon."** (the slow-time Feat description in `data.js`) A one-liner that is a sex joke and an accurate description of the mechanic.

6. **Flaccid** (status effect) The innocent clinical term. A legitimate word that is funny only in the player's ear.

### Takeaways

- The best existing lines share one shape: **flat setup, specific detail, no reaction to it.**
- The best **names and descriptions** share another: **a real sex, contraception or medical phrase, one word changed or one claim borrowed, and a mechanic that fits.**
- `ENEMY_INTRO` `what` lines are the right length for a moment screen. The `tip` lines are the over-explaining that Section 4 targets.
- Tier 2 and 3 barely exist in the live copy (closest: `born`, `gacha` and the egg-win lines). They are the main gap to fill.
