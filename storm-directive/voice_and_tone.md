# Spawn Prawn: Voice, Tone and Humour Guide

Rewritten 29 Oct 2026. Direction: **the game has a tone, not characters.** There is no narrator persona and no inner voice to write for. Every line is a label, a caption or a log entry in the game's own register: deadpan, clinical, juvenile with a straight face.

This supersedes the persona sections of the older guides (`redesign docs/VOICE_GUIDE.md` sections on the Scientist and the consciousness, `redesign docs/CONSCIOUSNESS.md`). Their rules on brevity, British spelling and progressive disclosure still agree with this one. The user writes all player-facing text; use this guide to write briefs and to judge drift.

---

## 1. The benchmark

These six are the tone. Everything else in this guide is a way of making more of them.

| Name | The real phrase | What it does in the game |
|---|---|---|
| **Premature Evangelation** | premature ejaculation | Guardian angels circle you and bless enemies early |
| **Hot Load** | hot load | +40% damage |
| **Big Load** | big load | Approved name, not yet in the game. The mechanic should be more of it (shots, size or payload) |
| **One Pump Chump** | one pump chump | Approved name, not yet in the game. The mechanic should be one shot, then spent |
| **Prawn Again** | born again | Memories of a past life surface as you level |
| **Latex Protection (98% effective)** | latex protection, 98% effective | A stretchy shield that flings bullets back. The 2% is the joke |

What they share, ranked by importance:

1. **The joke is the name.** The description is just the stat or the plain effect ("+40% damage."). Nothing explains the name.
2. **A real sex or contraception phrase, one word changed or none.** The player recognises it in under a second.
3. **The mechanic fits the phrase.** Angels bless early. Hot Load does more damage. Prawn Again remembers. If the mechanic does not earn the pun, it is just a pun.
4. **No speaker.** None of these depend on who is saying them. They read like a label on a vial, a card or a form.
5. **Crude in meaning, clinical on the page.** The line never winks, never adds an exclamation mark, never follows itself with a comment.

Quality test: say the name aloud. A small groan followed by a grin means keep it. If it needs a second sentence, rewrite it. If it is cute but does not describe the mechanic, rewrite it.

## 2. The register

**Deadpan lab paperwork about sex and death.** Flat, precise, short. The absurd is stated as routine. The reader supplies the reaction.

Ranked rules:

1. **State the absurd as routine.** No emphasis, no "wow", no exclamation marks (the one exception is in section 4).
2. **Precision is the joke.** The correct clinical word, a real number, a form name. "Approximately 4,000 body-lengths" beats "far".
3. **Real science underneath.** Every gag sits on a true fact. The player learns something by accident.
4. **Short.** One sentence, two at most. The joke is the last word.
5. **No speaker, no audience.** Lines are not said by anyone to anyone. No "I", no "we", no "me". No viewers, sponsors, staff, committees, colleagues or a scientist in the room. A caption, not a voice.
6. **Never game-aware.** No "this is a game", no "as you can see", no menus or tutorials named as such.
7. **Indifference over cruelty.** The copy does not hate the swimmer. It simply does not care.

**Always:** British spelling, brackets instead of em dashes, "damage type", "unstable reaction", "Feat" (you "perform" it), "swimmer" or "sample" as plain description.
**Never:** British slang, "element/elemental", "cast", memes, pop-culture references, first person, named or implied people.

## 3. Humour mechanics

### The three signature techniques (names and descriptions)

**1. The corrupted phrase.** Take a real phrase from sex, contraception or medicine, change one word (or none), and make the mechanic match the new meaning. The six benchmark names are all this.

1. One real phrase, one change. Recognised in under a second or dropped.
2. The mechanic must earn the pun.
3. Pick the plain word. The swap is mundane and slightly wrong, not clever.
4. Never gloss it. The description says what the thing does, in plain words.
5. Names first: weapons, Feats, Trophies, mutations, bosses, samples, achievements, statuses, reactions.
6. Budget: a handful per content batch. Plain odd nouns (Hiccup Scattergun, Bit of Sweetcorn, Stray Pube) still do most of the naming work.

**2. The borrowed disclaimer.** A real-world claim, statistic or label applied with a straight face. "98% effective." "Results may vary." "Consult your doctor." "Not suitable for under 18s." "Side effects include..."

1. Last words only. It ends the line.
2. The figure or label must look real. Accurate-looking beats jokey.
3. Nothing follows it.

**3. The innocent clinical term.** A genuine word, used correctly, that is funny only in the player's ear. **Flaccid** is the model. Also *tumescent*, *turgid*, *limp*, *spent*, *engorged*, *swollen*, *Lubricated*, *Soaking wet*.

1. Real term, correct use. A stretch is a pun, not this.
2. The line around it stays clinical. "Status: flaccid. Duration: 6 seconds."
3. Nothing reacts to it. The page does not hear the joke.
4. Best in statuses, stat labels, form fields and codex notes.

### Supporting mechanics (narration, cards, codex), ranked by how often to use them

1. **Bureaucratic framing.** Forms, quotas, risk assessments, sample IDs, filed paperwork. "Time has been rewound. Biology has filed a formal complaint."
2. **Understatement.** A catastrophe described as a minor inconvenience. "Nobody knows how they got in here."
3. **The true fact, delivered flat.** "One of the four hundred million. Not a threat on its own. There is never one on its own."
4. **The list that turns.** Two normal items, then one wrong one.
5. **The over-specific number.** "61% of..." beats "most".
6. **The re-label.** A game term redefined in the game's own flat logic. "It is not a crit. It is being believed."

Avoid: wordplay stacks (more than one twist per name), "random" humour, jokes that need setup the player has not seen, explaining the joke, two jokes in one line, puns on pop culture.

## 4. Volume: flat by default, loud almost never

The copy is flat about 98% of the time. A loud line is spent only on rare events and is always followed by a flat one.

| Volume | Used for | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **Flat** | Everything routine: tutorials, loot, deaths, menus, hits, boss intros | About 98% |
| **Loud** | Immaculate or Celestial drops, reaching the egg, first boss kill, an absurd chain reaction, a 1-in-1000 event | Under 2%. Players should remember each one. |

Rules:

1. **Earn it.** If it happens every run, it is not loud. Twice in one sitting means the trigger is too common.
2. **Crude must be specific.** One precise anatomical image beats five expletives. This is the only place an exclamation mark is allowed.
3. **Aimed at the situation, never at groups.** Bodies and biology are fair game. Real people, groups and tragedies are not.
4. **Recover flat.** The next line is routine, with no acknowledgement ("Logged." "Form 12B filed."). The flat recovery is half the joke.
5. **Loud is still speakerless.** "FOURTEEN YEARS AND NOTHING LIKE THAT IS IN THE LITERATURE." not "I have never seen...".

## 5. Progressive disclosure: less is more

**Name it, show it, and let the player discover how it works. The full explanation lives in the Field Guide (codex).**

| Layer | Where | Contains | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Show** | Cards, popups, HUD | The name and one flavour line. The effect is visible when it happens. | 1 short line |
| **2. Hint** | First-time cards only, only when needed | One sentence saying what to do, never why or how much | About 12 words |
| **3. Codex** | Field Guide, inspect cards, Tackle Box detail | Numbers, durations, formulas, interactions, the reaction chart | As long as needed |

Rules:

1. **The "learn it by doing" test.** If a player can work it out in one use, write nothing.
2. **No numbers on moment screens**, except the bare stat the player is choosing between ("+40% damage.").
3. **One mechanic per card.** Two mechanics means two cards, at least 25 seconds apart, or one is cut.
4. **Say what, never why.** "Soaking wet" yes. "Soaking wet doubles Static damage for 6 seconds" no.
5. **Never describe an effect the player is watching.**
6. **Never repeat.** A mechanic gets one hint, ever. "Skip tutorial" stays on every card.
7. **Instruct reluctantly.** If a line must instruct, one sentence ("Tap the thing. Yes, that one.").
8. **Codex entries can be generous.** Full numbers, the joke at full length and the real science belong there.

Exceptions (explain up front, briefly): anything that can lose progress or currency; controls the player cannot guess; hidden information they cannot infer from watching (why a boss is immune right now).

## 6. Per-screen rules

### Title, menus, settings
- Flat only. Plain labels, one dry subtitle at most.
- Setting hints: one factual sentence, no joke unless it is free.
- Reset and delete buttons: serious and plain. No joke before a destructive action.

### Tutorial and first-sighting cards (`tutorial.js`, `intro.js`, `statusintro.js`)
- Layers 1 and 2 only. One mechanic, one sentence, one dry line.
- Enemy first sightings (`ENEMY_INTRO`): the `what` line is the card. Tactical `tip` text belongs in the Field Guide.

### Loot and level-up cards (`genLoot`, `UI.openLoot`)
- Name plus one flavour line. The only number is the stat change on offer.
- No stacking rules, no "synergises with". Synergies are for the player to find and the codex to confirm.
- Common to Mythical: flat. Immaculate and Celestial: the loud line.

### Weapons, Feats, Trophies, mutations, samples
- **Name:** the joke (section 1 and 3).
- **Description:** what it visibly does, in plain words, then optionally one dry clause or a borrowed disclaimer.
- Never list numbers, scaling or reaction partners here. Feats are performed, never cast.
- Samples are labels on vials (number, letter, name: 1A "Baseline"). No personality attached to a sample; the name and its passive carry the joke.

### Damage types and unstable reactions
- Types get flat clinical names (Acid, Mucus, Static, Cooties, Oil, Water, Blood, Parasite).
- Reaction names are incident-report titles. A reaction in play gets a name flash, nothing more.
- The chart lives in the codex only.

### HUD and in-run narration (`SYSTEM_LINES`, campaign lines)
- Mostly silent. Lines fire on events, not timers, and never narrate what is on screen.
- Every line is a caption or log entry. No first person, no inner monologue, no one addressing the swimmer by name.
- Campaign levels use the same register as every other mode. Keep the setting a surprise until the level is beaten ("Level N", never the real name).
- Do not repeat a line within a run. Keep pools large.

### Bosses
- **Intro:** the file card. One fact, one understatement.
- **Mid-fight:** silence, or one dry line at an enrage.
- **Kill:** flat. First kill of a boss may be loud, then flat recovery.
- Each boss teaches one mechanic through play. No card explaining it.

### Death and win screens
- **Death:** flat always. State the cause precisely and without sympathy. "Cause of death: optimism."
- **Win (egg reached):** loud for the first win, flat after. Mode clears (Petri Dish) are flat.
- The sting is the flat line after the loud one.

### Field Guide, codex, inspect cards
- Thorough is allowed. Full stats, full interactions, the real science paragraph.
- Jokes may run longer, since the player chose to read.
- Locked entries stay vague and flat ("Not yet filed.").

### Achievements and DNA unlocks
- Flat names, flat descriptions. Corrupted phrases welcome.
- Do not spell out a condition in a way that removes the discovery, unless it is a stat milestone.

### Errors, missing keystore, crashes, save problems
- State the cause and the fix. No voice, no joke.

## 7. Copy checklist (run before any text ships)

1. Is there an "I", "we", "me", or a person in the room? Cut it or rewrite as a caption.
2. Can the player work it out by doing it once? Delete the line.
3. Is there a number or duration on a moment screen that is not the stat on offer? Move it to the codex.
4. More than one sentence? Cut it.
5. Does the joke need something the player has not seen? Rewrite it.
6. Is it loud? Check it fires under 2% of the time, and that the next line is flat.
7. "Element", "cast", slang, an exclamation mark outside a loud line, an em dash? Fix it.
8. Does the name describe the mechanic without the description's help? If not, rename or rewrite.

### B. Exceptions and remaining drift (audited 29 Oct 2026, v10.223)

The persona cut and the copy pass (v10.221 to v10.223) removed the narrator personas, the inner voice, the card quips and every first-person caption. What is left falls into three groups.

**Deliberate exception: enemies may speak.** Rival quotes and `KILL_LINES`, boss quotes (The Failed Experiment: "I know every move you are about to make. I made them first."), the joke-enemy decoy lines. The user kept these (29 Oct): an enemy taunting is a mechanic, not a narrator. The game itself still never says "I". Taunts stay short, in the enemy's own voice, and never address the player by name.

**HUD alerts.** Banners and float text are caps, with a colon instead of an exclamation mark ("THE RINSE: TAKE COVER", "OVERSHOT: HIT ITS BACK"). Caps are already loud.

### Takeaways

- The best names share one shape: **a real sex or contraception phrase, one word changed or one claim borrowed, and a mechanic that fits.**
- The best lines share another: **flat setup, specific detail, no reaction, nobody speaking.**
- The live pools now hold one voice: captions with nobody speaking. Enemies are the only things allowed to talk.
